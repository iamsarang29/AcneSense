# Initial reference sources

This is a starter bibliography for the reference and explanation layer. It is not a database of proven personal triggers. Keep the evidence type, date, population, and limitations attached to any passage indexed for retrieval.

## Guidelines and evidence reviews

1. **NICE, Acne vulgaris: management (NG198).** [Guideline overview](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG198) · [Recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng198/chapter/Recommendations). Includes skincare and diet guidance; the page reports a review date of 30 April 2026. NICE says there is not enough evidence to support specific diets for treating acne.
2. **NICE Evidence Review C: Dietary interventions for the treatment of acne vulgaris.** [NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK573052/). Includes the evidence and limitations considered for diet-related recommendations.
3. **NICE Evidence Review B: Skin care advice for people with acne vulgaris.** Find it through the [NG198 evidence reviews](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng198/evidence). Useful for grounding skincare-related explanations.
4. **Reynolds et al. (2024), Guidelines of care for the management of acne vulgaris.** [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38300170/). A professional clinical guideline; its main purpose is management, not determining an individual's triggers.

## Reviews and individual studies

5. **Baldwin & Tan (2021), Diet and acne: review of the evidence from 2009 to 2020.** [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33462816/). Use with its study-design mix and evidence limitations retained.
6. **Sleep Disturbances and Acne: A Comprehensive Review.** [Full text on PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12615111/). Potential sleep-related context; do not interpret reported associations as proof of causation.
7. **Chiarelli et al. (2015), Cosmetics for acne: indications and recommendations for an evidence-based approach.** [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25315288/). Background on cosmetic products; pair with current guidance and product-specific ingredient data where relevant.

## Textbooks (licensed access required)

- **Bolognia, Schaffer & Cerroni, Dermatology, 5th edition (2024).** [Publisher page](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/dermatology/bolognia/978-0-7020-8225-2). Broad dermatology background. Check the publisher's terms before digitizing or indexing book text.
- **Fitzpatrick's Dermatology, 9th edition.** [AccessMedicine book page](https://accessmedicine.mhmedical.com/book.aspx?bookID=2570). Licensed reference access; check terms before extracting or indexing content.

## Indexing notes

- Begin with guideline pages, public evidence reviews, and papers that permit the intended use. Do not upload scanned books or copyrighted full text without permission.
- Split sources into short, coherent passages. Store source title, URL, authors, publication/review date, source type, population, study design, exposure, outcome, and stated limitations beside each passage.
- Use semantic retrieval to find relevant reference passages for explanations. Keep personal daily logs as dated structured data for analysis; do not collapse them into one embedding and treat nearest-neighbor matches as trigger evidence.
- Preserve conflicting or uncertain findings instead of returning a single simplified verdict.
- Recheck guidelines and source pages periodically before relying on them; maintain a reviewed-on date in the eventual index.
